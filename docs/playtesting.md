# Playtesting Footsteps

Start a session with a question that could change one design decision. For the first social game: does the first elimination follow visible evidence about the floor, or does someone nominate simply because they can? Record the turn, the public information available just before nomination, the stated reason, the ballots, and how many turns the eliminated player sat out. Look at the board as the players saw it before reading the referee record.

For the next paper session, use the [two facilitator questions and the candidate opening rule](design-review.md#two-questions-for-the-facilitator).

In an early guided game, let players explain a disputed move before supplying the ruling. Observe whether they can tell the difference between entering and leaving a tile. In a later blind test, give a fresh group the rulebook and worked game and let them run setup and a round without the designer. Keep a list of where they asked for help and what they inferred. This adapts Kevin Brunner’s practice of recording observed confusion and distinguishing guided tests from blind rulebook tests. [Designer notes on playtesting](https://www.rok-games.com/post/designer-notes-itm-10-playtesting)

For simulations, state the board question, control policy, parameter grid, tile distribution, stopping rule and seed panel before interpreting the results. Keep the raw outcomes, exact reproduction command and one inspectable example. Report turns among winners alongside losses and trapped positions. For a late-game intervention, replay or regenerate a shared prefix, report what qualifies a position, how many starts qualify, and both directions of paired outcome change. A change to the policy, mix and exit at the same time needs separate follow-up comparisons.

After a session, record the observation separately from proposed fixes. Write an issue with a public event sequence or synthetic fixture, a design question and an observable acceptance criterion. Keep the selected rule and rationale in the rulebook or a decision note when the issue closes.

For model play, give mechanical findings and playing strength separate denominators. A model can lose every deal and still expose a bad timeout, an illegal prompt option, or a replay that changes under a quarter-turn. To claim a policy understands a choice, reuse the same private position, reverse the menu, and include a position where choosing the closest tile loses. Keep repeated presentations of one position together in the results.

A scripted player can isolate one part of a turn. For example, replace ballots while the model retains placement, discard, proposal and nomination. Record which decisions were scripted, what private view the policy received, and how discussion could affect it. A policy that ignores discussion can serve as a reproducible voting control. Measuring persuasion requires listeners whose decisions can respond to what was said.
