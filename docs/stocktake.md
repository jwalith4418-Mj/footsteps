# After the first persistent-seat studies

The runner can play complete solitaire and social games, survive compaction and retrieve attributed evidence. The most informative social result is still a mistake. A 9B listener endorsed a false accusation, then retrieved the relevant movement ballots and still voted to remove the accused. Separately, three players had a coherent discussion about a detour when the remaining turns already made EXIT unreachable. These give us concrete decisions to test without adding strategy advice. A win count alone would hide both behaviors. ## Next work

1. **Protect replay integrity and finish the supported seat migration.** A direct Python caller can mutate its placement coordinates after commit and change the replay. This is independent of the model work and has a small reproducer. For migration, exercise lost build and sealed-ballot responses and carry opt-in decision evidence into omniscient replay. The old and new clients share transport and action code. Remove a flag, helper or test once its last supported caller has moved and the archived experiment has a pinned reproduction path. [Replay integrity #27](https://github.com/othercriteria/footsteps/issues/27) · [Recovery #28](https://github.com/othercriteria/footsteps/issues/28) · [Private evidence #109](https://github.com/othercriteria/footsteps/issues/109) · [Migration #104](https://github.com/othercriteria/footsteps/issues/104)
2. **Measure whether a player understands who wins a proposed EXIT move.** Use the same legal position, vary the voter and proposer roles, include a nonterminal move, and score the predicted winner separately from the submitted ballot. It is a cheaper diagnostic than another unconstrained table. In parallel, the nomination terminology trial asks whether a seat understands that it is proposing a removal. Then test whether a voter retrieves the correct earlier ballot before speaking and acting. [EXIT ballot #101](https://github.com/othercriteria/footsteps/issues/101) · [Removal terminology #106](https://github.com/othercriteria/footsteps/issues/106) · [Evidence use #55](https://github.com/othercriteria/footsteps/issues/55)
